## Current Product

Verto is an AI-assisted innovation pipeline focused on quality under overload. It turns overwhelming employee idea intake into refined, explainable candidate sets for R&D review. Main product story is idea intake/refinement; campaign setup supports ground truth; Global Innovation Context is a supporting capability.

## Key Business Decisions

- Economic buyer: Head of Innovation / R&D leader.
- Champion: R&D Campaign Owner, also called an R&D Team Lead.
- Current product scope is the Idea Filter Pipeline. Verto clarifies, routes, ranks, and helps R&D select promising ideas as Potential Ideas. Potential Ideas leave the Verto pipeline and are, by default, ideas R&D intends to prototype or otherwise work on outside Verto. Verto does not yet manage the full idea-to-product pipeline after selection.
- R&D owns employee-facing topics and final decisions.
- R&D Managers own Company Context and Global Innovation Context approvals.
- R&D Managers can create any specific campaign and assign or change its R&D Campaign Owner. Changing owner after setup starts requires an R&D Manager.
- R&D Manager is also the default R&D Campaign Owner for the General Campaign.
- Only R&D Managers can add or remove R&D Campaign Owners for the General Campaign. Non-manager General Campaign owners have broad authority inside the General Campaign similar to an R&D Manager, but cannot manage General Campaign owner membership.
- R&D Campaign Owners own campaign setup, campaign AI question resolution, and Campaign Knowledge Report approval.
- Existing R&D Campaign Owners can create a new specific campaign only when assigning themself as owner. Once assigned, that owner controls setup and lifecycle gates unless an R&D Manager changes ownership.
- R&D Team Members can answer AI questions and contribute draft changes directly into campaign setup, but those answers stay Pending Owner Approval until the R&D Campaign Owner approves them. They can draft or propose a campaign, but it needs R&D Manager or assigned R&D Campaign Owner approval before becoming real setup ground truth.
- R&D Campaign Owners can add/remove R&D Team Members for their own campaign. R&D Managers can also add/remove members when needed. R&D Team Members cannot manage membership. Removing a member stops future access/contribution, but keeps past draft history and audit trail.
- Added R&D Team Members can see the campaign R&D workspace, including setup, Campaign Knowledge Report, AI questions, draft answers, idea groups, rankings, review packets, variants, employee identity shown in review packets, and future/inactive returns for that campaign. They can recommend or draft actions, but cannot approve reports, lifecycle gates, Potential Ideas, return decisions, owner-only context overrides, or membership.
- AI suggests, clarifies, classifies, and explains; it does not replace R&D judgment.
- R&D should be able to understand important AI decisions where the product exposes them. In the MVP, explanations should appear in the app only where already specified, such as rank reasons or assumption-based routing. If no in-app surface has been specified for a decision, capture the explanation in a demo AI decision log instead of adding new UI.
- Employee identity is visible to R&D for collaboration after selection, but must not drive AI evaluation.
- Specific Campaign intake audience defaults to all seeded/demo employees in the MVP. R&D Campaign Owner chooses employee-facing title/context before opening intake. MVP does not need complex audience targeting by department, team, or location.
- Employee submission portal shows General Campaign always. Specific campaigns show for submission only when Intake Open. Intake Scheduled, Ready To Open, setup, closed, review, and ended campaigns are not shown for submission in the MVP. If no specific campaign is open, employees still submit to General Campaign.
- Employee-facing campaign preview shows campaign title, short public context/prompt, optional examples of useful ideas, and submission deadline when relevant. It hides internal minimum review packet, out-of-scope rule names, ranking criteria, Global Innovation Context, and R&D strategy notes. R&D Campaign Owner can edit public title/prompt/examples after intake opens. General Campaign Owners can edit the General Campaign public title/prompt/examples, and R&D Manager can edit because the manager is default General Campaign owner. In the MVP, preview-only edits do not trigger AI consistency checks or routing rechecks; existing ideas keep the preview version used at submission for audit/source trace only. No dedicated preview version-history UI or diff UI in MVP. General Campaign also has a minimal preview such as General Ideas, a short prompt to submit ideas that do not fit a current campaign, optional examples, and no deadline.
- Employee managers should not appear in the MVP idea review packet.
- Employee department or team may appear in the idea review packet when already available, but only as R&D context. AI should not use department or team for ranking, filtering, or routing unless the campaign context explicitly asks for department-specific ideas.
- Manager collaboration is real-world workflow after selection, not MVP demo core.

## Core Flows

- R&D campaign interview uses question mode to establish campaign common ground, intentional ambiguity, minimum review packet, out-of-scope patterns, and conflict resolution.
- Question Mode follows the rules in `docs/QUESTION_MODE_RULES.md`: ask one focused question at a time, recommend an answer when possible, challenge unclear terms against the glossary, inspect existing docs before asking, and record resolved decisions.
- AI should use scoped RAG-style context: retrieve only context relevant to the current decision. For campaign-specific idea evaluation, use current campaign context, relevant idea source material, and minimal related context. Do not feed unrelated campaigns or the whole knowledge base into every AI call. Active campaigns should not directly reroute ideas from one active campaign to another; cross-campaign reuse happens through the General Campaign.
- Campaign setup prioritizes AI-guided conversation first. R&D can inspect a structured setup view, but stored changes still go through AI interpretation and are reflected in the campaign knowledge report.
- Campaign setup flow currently uses Topic, Intent, Review Packet, Rules & Memory, and Final Review. Publish is not a full setup step; Final Review contains the campaign knowledge report and the one-button open-intake action once hard blockers are resolved.
- Specific campaigns use the canonical current-scope lifecycle: Draft, Setup In Progress, Setup Review, Ready To Open, Intake Scheduled, Intake Open, Intake Closed, Review In Progress, Review Complete, Retrospective, and Ended. There is no Archived status. Intake Paused is planned future lifecycle status, but it is out of current scope. The General Campaign does not use this lifecycle because it is always open and long-running.
- Campaign lifecycle gates are owner-controlled. R&D Team Members can contribute during setup and review, and AI can recommend status changes with reasons, but only the R&D Campaign Owner can approve business gate changes such as Ready To Open, Intake Open, Intake Closed, Review Complete, Retrospective, and Ended. Scheduled automation can open intake or close intake at the approved end date only after the owner approved the schedule. When scheduled intake time ends, new submissions stop, existing submitted ideas keep processing, existing employee clarification links stay active until their own 30-day expiry, and R&D can still review ready ideas. AI cannot independently open, close, or end a specific campaign outside owner-approved gates or schedules.
- R&D can review ideas that are Ready for R&D Review while campaign intake is still open. Review In Progress means the formal review phase after intake closes, not the first moment R&D is allowed to inspect or decide on an idea.
- When intake closes, the campaign moves to Review In Progress if any unreviewed ideas that are Ready for R&D Review remain. If only clarification or routing work remains, it stays Intake Closed until those finish. Review Complete requires R&D Campaign Owner approval because it means R&D is done with that campaign's review work.
- Before Review Complete, every idea that is Ready for R&D Review in that campaign must have an explicit outcome: Potential Idea, returned to Future Opportunity, or returned to Inactive Idea. If any Ready for R&D Review idea has no outcome, AI blocks Review Complete and shows the owner which ideas still need a decision. Ideas still in clarification or routing follow separate campaign-end return rules.
- Retrospective is optional for ending a campaign. After Review Complete, the R&D Campaign Owner can move directly to Ended without running a retrospective. AI should offer a small retrospective Question Mode, but the owner can skip it. If skipped, no Global Innovation Context proposal is created from that campaign at that time. The owner can later start the retrospective from ended campaign history if needed.
- Ended campaigns remain searchable and visible in campaign history. R&D can inspect setup, final Campaign Knowledge Report, idea lineage, outcomes, and retrospective status. Ended campaigns do not accept new intake, pull new ideas from General Campaign, or receive new review decisions except optional retrospective later. If an employee clarification answer arrives after the campaign has ended, the answer becomes source material on the shared idea in General Campaign and AI evaluates it under General Campaign rules, not the ended campaign. Ended campaigns should not be reopened in current scope. If R&D wants to continue the same theme, they create a new campaign using the ended campaign as track record evidence or cloned setup context, while the ended campaign remains unchanged.
- Agent question queue is global across the whole campaign setup. It prioritizes setup hard blockers first, then setup warnings, then clarity questions. Non-blocking questions can be skipped or dismissed, but skipped assumptions remain visible in the campaign knowledge report.
- The owner attention queue is the R&D Campaign Owner's review list. It gathers Pending Owner Approval answers, AI-detected conflicts, setup hard blockers, warnings needing owner judgment, and proposed campaign context overrides. When an item is unclear, AI follows up with the R&D Campaign Owner in question mode until the decision is clear enough to record.
- Setup hard blockers prevent opening intake. Setup warnings do not block opening intake and remain visible in the campaign knowledge report.
- Manual/structured setup edits should not trigger live checking while R&D types. AI re-check runs after Save Draft or explicit AI check. AI check results appear inside the agent question queue.
- Campaign knowledge reports are campaign-specific HTML reports of what AI understands and will follow. They are not directly editable; corrections go through AI conversation.
- The R&D Campaign Owner may approve a campaign-only override when Company Context or Global Innovation Context conflicts with campaign intent. The override is recorded in the Campaign Knowledge Report. It does not change Company Context or Global Innovation Context unless the R&D Manager separately approves that broader change.
- Employee idea interview also uses question mode, but only when the idea lacks required detail.
- Anonymous idea submission is not part of the MVP. R&D needs employee identity for potential follow-up after selection, and clarification links need a known submitter. AI must still not use employee identity for ranking, filtering, or routing.
- Employees may submit more than one idea to the same campaign. Each submission should become a separate idea record unless AI detects it is clearly a duplicate or variant of an existing idea. Variants should be linked, not merged.
- Employees can save private idea drafts before submitting. Drafts belong only to the employee; R&D cannot see them, AI does not process them, R&D does not see draft counts, they do not count as submissions, and they have no clarification links. Draft becomes real idea only after Submit. A specific-campaign draft stays until employee deletes it, submits it, or that campaign closes intake. A General Campaign draft stays until employee deletes or submits it. Deleted drafts leave no R&D-facing, AI-facing, campaign, review, stats, or product audit record. No email/push when a draft becomes unavailable due to intake close; if employee is viewing it, show campaign intake closed.
- Initial idea submission stays lightweight. Required fields are short idea title and free-text idea description. Optional fields: problem/opportunity, expected benefit, rough evidence/example, supporting link. AI asks one clarification batch later when the campaign minimum review packet needs more. Employee file uploads are future scope unless implementation time allows; MVP supports links first.
- Each employee submission chooses one visible destination: one Intake Open specific campaign or General Campaign. Employees cannot choose multiple campaigns for the same idea in the MVP. If an employee submits to an active specific campaign, AI should not automatically detach the idea from that campaign or route it away just because it appears to fit another campaign or the General Campaign better. It stays associated with the submitted campaign until R&D makes an explicit outcome decision or the campaign reaches Ended and the campaign-end sweep returns remaining non-Potential ideas to the General Campaign. Same-idea submissions to another campaign are handled by duplicate/variant rules.
- After submitting, employee sees simple confirmation with idea title/reference ID and a note that AI or R&D may ask follow-up if needed. MVP has no employee-facing status tracking page, routing/result labels, rank, review outcome, or promise that R&D will review the idea.
- Employees should not directly edit their original idea after submission in the MVP. If AI needs more information, the employee clarifies through the clarification link instead.
- R&D should not edit an employee's original submitted idea text. The original submission should remain immutable for audit. R&D can mark outcomes, approve campaign-specific clarity, and make review decisions.
- AI can generate an R&D-Facing Idea Summary for review. It is separate from the immutable original submission and should cite or trace back to the original submission and any employee clarifications. It is internal R&D review material and should not be visible to employees. R&D should not directly edit the summary; if it is wrong, any R&D user with review packet access can ask AI to regenerate it with traceability. Summary regeneration is text-only, does not require owner approval, and should not affect routing or ranking. Regenerated summaries should keep lightweight version history, including prior summary versions, who requested regeneration, when, and a brief AI reason. R&D should respect employee answers that came through user clarification and should not override clarified source meaning. If R&D believes AI misunderstood the source, the correction should be framed as an AI interpretation issue, not a change to the employee's answer.
- If the same employee submits the exact same idea more than once, AI should keep both submission records for audit, mark the newer submission as a duplicate of the older idea, and avoid creating multiple review packets.
- If different employees submit the same or very similar idea, AI should treat them as related submissions or variants rather than a pure duplicate. The system should keep each employee's submission record, link them under one idea family, and create one review packet that shows multiple submitters or repeated signal.
- R&D may see employee names in an idea family as contact context before selecting a Potential Idea, but AI must not use employee identity for ranking, filtering, or routing.
- Employee clarification uses emailed conversation link. When AI routes an idea to Needs Employee Clarification, AI automatically creates the clarification batch, sends the employee clarification notice, and opens the 30-day timer. R&D does not manually send freeform clarification messages or resend timed-out links in the MVP, and owner approval is not required before AI sends the questions. Sent questions, send time, expiry time, and later employee answer remain visible to R&D users with campaign access as source/audit material. MVP does not need an R&D early-close or cancel path for a sent clarification link; strong scoped context and the one-batch rule should make clarification good enough for the hackathon version. Once sent, the clarification follows the normal employee answer or 30-day expiry path. MVP uses secure magic link without login as demo simplification; enterprise version should use SSO, directory permissions, link expiry, and audit logging.
- Employee clarification allows text answers and optional supporting link. Employee file uploads are not required for MVP clarification. MVP does not need link validation at submission or clarification time. MVP allows one clarification batch per idea per campaign context. Campaign Context changes do not allow another employee clarification batch inside the same specific campaign. AI asks focused questions once, then routes using the answer plus reasonable assumptions if still imperfect. If the same shared idea is later pulled into a different campaign context, that later campaign may ask its own one clarification batch only when reused idea clarification rules allow it. If a supporting link is inaccessible or unclear during the open batch, AI may ask about it inside that same batch; after answer or expiry, AI marks evidence unavailable instead of starting another employee follow-up round.
- After an employee answers clarification, AI checks whether critical Minimum Review Packet information is now satisfied. If yes, the idea may become Ready for R&D Review with noncritical unknowns or assumptions shown, and AI should update the review board immediately so the idea appears in the correct queue/group. The R&D board should show a small source trace explaining why the idea became Ready for R&D Review by mapping the relevant employee clarification answer to the packet item it satisfied. This source trace should not become a large explanation UI and should not require owner approval. This immediate placement update should not force a full group rerank unless a normal rerank trigger applies. If critical information is still missing, AI does not ask a second clarification batch and does not place the idea in normal R&D review. It routes the idea to Future Opportunity if still promising, or Inactive Idea if weak/poor fit, with a recorded reason such as insufficient clarity after clarification.
- Ideas in Needs Employee Clarification should appear to R&D in a separate Awaiting Clarification queue/count, not inside ranked review groups and not mixed with ideas that are Ready for R&D Review. R&D can inspect the initial idea, what AI asked, expiry date, and answer once it arrives. AI should provisionally rank/sort this queue using only the initial submitted idea and pre-clarification context. This rank is for scanning only, lower confidence than Ready for R&D Review ranking, and cannot be used for Potential/Future/Inactive decisions until clarification is answered or expires.
- Employee clarification hides internal criteria, scoring, ranking, review outcomes, routing status, related employee ideas, sensitive out-of-scope names, strategy notes, Global Innovation Context, and internal labels such as Potential Idea, Future Opportunity, Inactive Idea, and Assumption-Based Routing.
- AI can stop employee interview early if idea clearly matches an R&D-marked out-of-scope pattern.
- Pre-R&D routing happens before R&D review: campaign fit, related variants, future opportunity/out-of-scope/ready/needs-calibration states. For an idea submitted directly to an active specific campaign, pre-R&D routing should not automatically remove the idea from that campaign even when AI thinks the idea has poor fit or better fit elsewhere. Rerouting away from the submitted campaign happens only through an explicit R&D outcome decision or when the specific campaign reaches Ended and the campaign-end sweep returns remaining non-Potential ideas to the General Campaign.
- If Campaign B starts while Campaign A intake is active, Campaign B pulls matching ideas from the General Campaign only. It should not take ideas directly from Campaign A. When Campaign A ends, leftover ideas return to the General Campaign, then Campaign B may pull matching ideas from the General Campaign through normal campaign idea pull-in.
- Idea classification means grouping ideas into meaningful categories or clusters, such as vegan food ideas, packaging ideas, CPU upgrade ideas, or RAM upgrade ideas. It can be campaign-specific, global, or both. AI should create, choose, assign, and routinely manage appropriate groups for R&D review; R&D can rename, merge, or split groups when needed, but that should not be normal workflow. It is different from pre-R&D routing.
- General Campaign classification is for filtering and discovery, not review ranking. Groups should represent fundamentally similar ideas, with AI fitting ideas into existing groups before creating new groups.
- General Campaign classification should default to a 100-group cap to prevent category explosion, configurable later by General Campaign owners. When the cap is full and a truly unique idea needs a new group, AI should pick the smallest existing group by idea count, redistribute those ideas into the remaining groups where they fit, remove that group, and create the new group. This does not require owner approval by default. AI should keep an audit log when it dissolves a group, including old group name, reason, moved ideas, destination groups, timestamp, and context version. AI should not send push notifications for General Campaign group dissolution.
- Idea groups are AI-managed review lenses, not taxonomy objects R&D must maintain. The idea remains the primary artifact; group names matter less than helping R&D find, compare, and review relevant ideas.
- Single-idea groups are allowed only when useful: high-value, novel, outside existing groups, low-volume campaign, or important enough that the owner should not miss it. Ordinary lone ideas should stay in broader emerging-theme or other groups.
- Emerging themes should be visible but low priority. They rank above out-of-scope idea groups but below stronger campaign-fit groups.
- R&D-out-of-scope should be a reason inside Inactive Ideas, not a separate final bucket, and should appear at the very bottom of the Inactive Ideas list.
- Inactive Ideas should not compete with viable idea groups in the main review flow. Main review shows count and top reasons; inactive ideas view groups by reason, shows matched evidence/rules, and lets R&D reclassify when AI is wrong.
- Future Opportunities should be separate from Inactive Ideas because future opportunities may be valuable later. Ideas that do not fit a current campaign should return to the Always-Open Campaign, also called the General Campaign, as either Future Opportunities or Inactive Ideas. Both views live under the General Campaign. If an idea fits Company Context or Global Innovation Context but not the current campaign, it usually becomes a Future Opportunity.
- Inactive Ideas are not useful under current known context, but they are kept for audit, search, and reclassification.
- Future Opportunities live in the Always-Open/General Campaign with lineage back to the campaign that saved them. They return to active campaign work only when AI or R&D later finds enough campaign fit and clarity for them to satisfy the new campaign's Minimum Review Packet. Until then, they stay in Future Opportunities.
- Ideas returned to the Always-Open Campaign should keep returned idea lineage: original campaign, reason it did not fit, context version used, future opportunity/inactive outcome, related ideas, and related groups.
- Idea records should be reused across campaigns rather than copied. When a new campaign opens or is set up, AI should pull in matching ideas from the Always-Open Campaign, looking at Future Opportunities first, then General Ideas that are Ready for R&D Review. Pulled ideas should stay as one shared idea record in General Campaign with an association to the pulling campaign. Inactive Ideas should not be pulled unless a recheck has moved them out of inactive first. Matching ideas can be associated with the campaign without requiring R&D Campaign Owner pre-approval.
- AI should pull a reused idea into a campaign only when the existing information is enough to establish campaign fit and qualifies the idea for that campaign's routing. If there is not enough information to establish fit in the first place, AI should not pull the idea from the General Campaign. After a reused idea is pulled, AI may ask the employee for more clarification only to enrich the campaign review packet when the idea is within the reused idea clarification window and the employee is still active. If extra clarification does not arrive, the idea should stay in the pulling campaign and use existing information with lower-confidence or assumption markers where needed. Missing extra clarification should not send the idea back to the General Campaign or mark it as Needs Critical Clarity.
- For reused ideas, AI should only ask the original employee for clarification when the idea is within the reused idea clarification window and the employee is still active. Default window: six months from original submission, configurable later. Reused idea clarification follows the same one-batch, 30-day clarification timeout, and one day-23 reminder rules as other employee clarification. If older or employee inactive, do not send employee clarification. If critical information is missing and cannot be clarified through an allowed source, the reused idea should not enter normal R&D review.
- Ideas can be pulled from the Always-Open Campaign into campaign routing as candidates, but they become normal R&D review work only after they satisfy the campaign's Minimum Review Packet and become Ready for R&D Review. Ready for R&D Review is an information gate, not a quality judgment, rank, selection, or claim that the idea is good. Noncritical unknowns may be shown as unknowns or assumptions; critical missing information means the idea is not Ready for R&D Review.
- Candidate ideas that are missing critical information should use the future opportunity reason Needs Critical Clarity. They should be treated as Future Opportunities and should not create separate effects, owner attention, normal R&D review work, or review group ranking impact. The R&D Campaign Owner can supply or approve missing campaign-specific clarity. R&D Team Members can draft it, but AI should not promote the idea to Ready for R&D Review or reroute it into normal R&D review until the owner approves the draft.
- Opening an idea group should show an idea group summary before individual idea packets. The summary explains the shared theme, count, strongest candidates, repeated patterns, variants, missing information, and out-of-scope risk.
- AI should rank idea groups by expected benefit to the R&D Campaign Owner so the owner can review top to bottom or explore as needed. Ranking should consider campaign fit, potential value, idea quality, urgency, evidence strength, novelty, feasibility signals, and unresolved risk.
- Ranking changes do not need separate explanation by default.
- General Campaign review should rank ideas directly, not groups, because broad intake may create too many groups.
- Idea group ranking should not recalculate just because the owner opens the review board. Valid rerank triggers are review threshold reached, scheduled daily/weekly in-app review digest, major Campaign Context change, enough new ideas arriving to shift priority, or campaign time running out. A clarification answer that makes one idea Ready for R&D Review updates board placement immediately, but does not force a full group rerank unless a normal rerank trigger applies.
- AI should rank ideas subjectively using documented context and visible judgment criteria, not rigid formulas, employee seniority, or popularity. In specific campaigns, AI may rank ideas inside a group so R&D can inspect stronger candidate packets first. In General Campaign review, AI ranks individual ideas directly using fit with Company Context, fit with Global Innovation Context, expected business or R&D value, novelty, feasibility signal, evidence quality, and urgency or strategic timing. Repeated signal from multiple employees may lightly raise confidence or urgency, but should not become popularity voting. Each ranked idea should show 2-4 short rank reasons and no exact numeric score by default. Lower-ranked ideas remain visible and R&D judgment remains final.
- Idea classification can happen on raw submissions as a rough grouping, then become stronger after employee clarification. If clarification changes meaning, AI can move the idea to a different group and record why.
- One idea may appear in multiple classification groups, with AI choosing one campaign-relative primary group for dashboard counting, but it should produce one R&D review packet and be reviewed once within a campaign context.
- A shared idea associated with multiple campaigns may be reviewed once per campaign context because each campaign may have different review criteria. If any associated campaign marks it as Potential Idea, it exits all campaign queues.
- When Campaign Context changes after intake opens, AI rechecks unreviewed idea routing only. Already reviewed R&D decisions stay tied to the context version used at review time. If routing changes, AI records old reason, new reason, and the context version that caused the change. Context changes do not reset employee clarification limits for the same specific campaign; AI rechecks with existing answers, current context, and assumptions, or asks the R&D Campaign Owner in Question Mode.
- When General Campaign Minimum Review Packet changes, AI rechecks unreviewed General Campaign ideas, Future Opportunities, and Inactive Ideas only. Already reviewed decisions stay tied to the old packet/context version.
- For specific campaigns, Company Context or Global Innovation Context changes do not trigger routing recheck by themselves. Recheck starts only after the R&D Campaign Owner resolves a context change alert by changing Campaign Context or recording a campaign context override.
- For General Campaign, Company Context or Global Innovation Context changes directly trigger routing recheck for unreviewed General Ideas, Future Opportunities, and Inactive Ideas because General Campaign uses those contexts to decide company fit.
- Routing recheck changes stay internal by default. Employees are contacted only when recheck creates a need for clarification, or when a separate campaign communication decision requires notification.
- General Campaign recheck should store audit history and update counts/views without immediate owner notification by default. AI should notify General Campaign owners through a scheduled in-app digest only in the MVP. MVP should not send R&D owner digest email or push notifications; email is reserved for employee clarification links.
- Scheduled in-app review digest cadence is fixed in the MVP, not configurable: active specific campaigns use a daily in-app digest, and the General Campaign uses a weekly in-app digest.
- R&D Team Members with access to the relevant campaign workspace can read the same scheduled in-app review digest as the R&D Campaign Owner, but digest-linked actions that require owner authority remain owner-only. For General Campaign, General Campaign Owners see the owner digest; R&D Team Members see the digest only if they have access to the General Campaign workspace.
- Scheduled in-app review digest content should stay compact and actionable: new ideas that became Ready for R&D Review, ideas still Awaiting Clarification near expiry, major routing changes, and rerank summary only when rerank happened. No long AI essay. Each item links to the relevant idea, queue, source trace, or routing detail.
- Scheduled in-app review digest items are not individually dismissible in the MVP because digest is a generated summary, not a task inbox. Items disappear naturally when next digest replaces old digest or when linked state changes.
- MVP shows latest digest only, with no separate digest history page. Underlying events remain available through idea records, routing history, source traces, and audit logs.
- Potential Idea is the happy-path selected final state for an idea that has passed out of the Idea Filter Pipeline. R&D can select a Potential Idea from a specific campaign review or from General Campaign review. By default, it means R&D selected the idea for prototyping or other work outside Verto. R&D should choose one lightweight fixed system reason tag when marking an idea as Potential Idea, with an optional note. MVP reason tags can include high value, strong fit, novel, quick prototype, strategic timing, or customer/employee pain. Once marked as Potential Idea, the idea leaves normal review queues and rankings. It remains searchable with lineage and outcome history, but no longer competes with unreviewed ideas. Potential Ideas are excluded from future campaign pull-in. Verto should not reopen a Potential Idea back into the Idea Filter Pipeline; whether the later prototype or work succeeds is outside current scope.
- Only the relevant owner can mark an idea as Potential Idea: the R&D Campaign Owner for a specific campaign, or a General Campaign Owner for General Campaign review. An R&D Team Member can recommend marking an idea as Potential Idea, but owner approval is required.
- General Campaign review may still select a shared idea as Potential Idea while specific campaign review is pending. If any associated campaign or the General Campaign marks a shared idea as Potential Idea, the idea should be unplugged from other campaign routing and review queues because it has left the Idea Filter Pipeline globally. Other associated campaign owners should not receive a notification by default. If they later open the idea or history, they should see that it was marked as Potential Idea elsewhere.
- Employees should not receive an automatic Verto notification when their idea is marked as Potential Idea in the MVP. R&D may contact the employee separately outside Verto if they want to prototype or work on the idea with them.
- Employees should not receive automatic Verto notifications when their idea becomes a Future Opportunity or Inactive Idea in the MVP. Verto should not create employee-facing rejection or internal status messaging for idea outcomes.
- Unused reviewed ideas return to the General Campaign as Future Opportunities or Inactive Ideas, depending on whether they may be useful later under known context. These are the only return choices for reviewed ideas that are not selected as Potential Ideas. R&D should choose one lightweight fixed system reason tag when returning an unused reviewed idea, with an optional note. MVP reason tags can include not now, insufficient value, poor fit, too unclear, out of scope, or duplicate/covered. AI does not need to suggest the return destination or reason tag by default.
- Only the relevant owner can return an unused reviewed idea to Future Opportunity or Inactive Idea: the R&D Campaign Owner for a specific campaign, or a General Campaign Owner for General Campaign review. An R&D Team Member can recommend the return decision, but owner approval is required.
- For shared ideas associated with multiple campaigns, returning an unused reviewed idea to Future Opportunity or Inactive Idea is campaign-context-specific. That return decision should not block another associated campaign from reviewing the same shared idea unless the idea becomes Potential Idea elsewhere. The shared idea record should keep all campaign-specific outcomes in lineage.
- If R&D thinks an employee answer is wrong, unrealistic, or impractical, that judgment belongs in the R&D review decision or reason tag. It should not rewrite the employee's answer or source meaning.
- R&D internal notes are out of scope for the MVP hackathon version. The MVP should rely on review decisions, fixed reason tags, optional reason notes, and owner approvals instead of implementing a separate internal note feature.
- Optional reason notes are small text fields attached to R&D review decisions or reason tags. They are decision metadata, not a separate internal note feature, and do not need search, collaboration, AI summarization, or standalone note management in the MVP.
- If a specific campaign ends with ideas that were Ready for R&D Review but never reviewed, those ideas return to General Campaign as Future Opportunities by default, with lineage back to the ended campaign and reason tag not reviewed before campaign ended. If clearly poor fit under broader known context, the R&D Campaign Owner can return one as an Inactive Idea instead.
- When a specific campaign reaches Ended, AI runs a campaign-end sweep so no non-Potential idea remains orphaned inside the ended campaign. Needs Employee Clarification ideas return to General Campaign and keep the same 30-day timer. If the employee answers after the campaign ended but before the clarification link expires, AI evaluates the answer under General Campaign rules and routes the shared idea as a General Campaign item: Future Opportunity, Inactive Idea, or Ready for R&D Review in General Campaign. If that late clarification makes the idea Ready for R&D Review in General Campaign, AI updates the General Campaign board and counts immediately, but notifies General Campaign owners only through the next scheduled in-app digest. Unfinished routing or candidate work returns to General Campaign with lineage and current best status. Potential Ideas stay out of the Idea Filter Pipeline globally.
- The 30-day clarification timeout applies to all ideas in Needs Employee Clarification. The clarification email and page should show the exact expiry date so the employee knows when the link closes. If one stays there for 30 days without being filled, AI closes the clarification link, judges the idea based on current state and reasonable assumptions, then routes it to Future Opportunity or Inactive Idea. After timeout, the clarification link is no longer accessible, so there is no late employee response path for that link. R&D should not resend a new clarification link for the same timed-out clarification in the MVP. After an employee answers a clarification batch, AI routes with that answer and assumptions if needed; no second employee clarification round in the same campaign context, including after Campaign Context changes.
- Employee-facing timeout messaging should gently say that if clarification is not received by the expiry date, the idea may be routed based on the information already provided. It should not expose internal labels such as Future Opportunity, Inactive Idea, or Assumption-Based Routing.
- The system should send one clarification reminder around day 23 of the 30-day clarification window. It should not send repeated reminders for the same clarification request.
- Expired clarification links should show a read-only expired page with expired message, idea title/reference, and expiry date. It should include no form fields, no resubmit button, and no request-new-link action.
- If a specific campaign ends with ideas still in Needs Employee Clarification, those ideas return to General Campaign and remain in Needs Employee Clarification until the same 30-day timeout or employee response resolves them. Any response after the specific campaign has ended is evaluated under General Campaign rules only.
- Assumption-Based Routing should be visibly recorded when AI routes an idea after clarification did not arrive. It should list what AI assumed, why those assumptions were reasonable, and which routing decision they supported. Ideas routed this way should be treated as lower confidence in ranking than similarly valuable fully clarified ideas, but they should not be hidden.
- Post-campaign R&D interview is a lightweight retrospective Question Mode session that only the R&D Campaign Owner can start for a specific campaign. It captures what R&D reviewed, marked as Potential Ideas, returned as Future Opportunities, returned as Inactive Ideas, or marked out-of-scope, and why.
- Learnings can be campaign-specific, become part of Global Innovation Context, or both; R&D chooses scope through the retrospective flow.
- Campaign-specific exceptions should normally be considered for Global Innovation Context during the post-campaign R&D interview, not during ordinary live campaign setup.
- If an R&D Campaign Owner wants a campaign exception to become company-wide guidance, AI creates a Global Context Change Proposal for R&D Manager approval instead of changing Company Context or Global Innovation Context directly.
- When Company Context or Global Innovation Context changes, AI runs a Context Impact Check against active and draft Campaign Context. If likely impact exists on an active campaign, active campaign owners may receive non-blocking Context Change Alerts. If likely impact exists on a draft campaign, it becomes setup work in the Agent Question Queue or setup review. These are quality-of-life notices only: they do not block intake, require review, or overwrite Campaign Context. In the Owner Attention Queue, alerts rank below setup hard blockers but above setup warnings.
- Draft context impact can become a setup warning when launch remains coherent, or a setup hard blocker when the change creates an unresolved contradiction in campaign ground truth, review packet requirements, or out-of-scope handling.
- Context Impact Check can produce No Impact, which creates no alert and stores audit only.
- Context Change Alert resolutions should use fixed outcomes: No Impact, update Campaign Context, create campaign context override, ask AI follow-up in question mode, or escalate to R&D Manager. No Impact means irrelevant or already covered; campaign context override means relevant but intentionally not followed for this campaign.
- Context Change Alerts do not automatically block active campaigns after intake opens. AI may recommend closing intake early for severe contradictions, but the R&D Campaign Owner decides.
- Company Context is optional reference context. Setup starts with company name, then R&D/admin chooses public web fetch or manual description/uploads. AI generates an HTML report, asks conflict/clarity questions when needed, and the report must be approved by an R&D Manager before use. MVP supports public sources and uploads only. If Company Context is not set up, company reference context is empty.
- Company Context is reference context, not an overwrite rule. If it conflicts with campaign setup, resolve inside AI Q&A and record the campaign-specific decision in the campaign knowledge report. General global idea-intake rules live in the Always-Open Campaign.
- Always-Open Campaign is also called General Campaign.
- General Idea is a temporary intake and routing state for a new idea submitted to the Always-Open/General Campaign. It is not a final long-term bucket. AI should route it to needs employee clarification, Future Opportunity, Inactive Idea, lowest-priority General Campaign review, or later into a specific campaign when it becomes Ready for R&D Review for that campaign. When no specific campaign exists, AI uses Company Context, Global Innovation Context, and always-open campaign rules rather than inventing campaign-specific criteria.
- General Campaign has a General Campaign Minimum Review Packet because R&D may review ideas directly from General Campaign when no specific campaign is active. It is broad and lowest review priority. AI uses Company Context, Global Innovation Context, and always-open campaign rules to decide whether the idea matches company vision enough for general review. General Campaign owners can manage this packet. Packet changes trigger routing recheck for unreviewed General Campaign ideas only.
- Company Context setup is its own admin flow, linked from Always-Open Campaign setup. Always-Open shows whether Company Context is approved, missing, or needs approval, but does not own Company Context.
